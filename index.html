Weekly plan

Five days, Monday to Friday, 2.5 hours each. Weekends off: that is when the muscle actually gets built. The cardio hour is in every session, which puts you at 300 minutes a week, double the ADA floor.

SunOff. Same walk.

Why the body is split this way

Every muscle you train needs about 48 hours to repair and grow, and growing is where the blood sugar benefit comes from. The ADA's guidance is resistance work 2 to 3 days a week per muscle on non-consecutive days; five gym days are fine as long as no muscle is hit two days in a row. So the week rotates through body parts, each muscle gets two sessions (one heavy on machines, one lighter with free weights), and the cardio hour runs daily since it does not need the same recovery.

  • Day A, chest and arms. Machine press first for heavy, safe loading, then the dumbbell version to teach balance. Chest presses already work the triceps, so the arm work piggybacks on warm muscles. Small arm muscles go last so they do not give out before the big lifts.
  • Day B, lower body on machines. Legs and glutes are the biggest muscles you have, which makes this the most important lifting day for glucose. Leg press first, then the single-joint machines. Heaviest loads of the week.
  • Day C, back. Lats, upper back, rear shoulders, plus a one-arm dumbbell row so each side works alone. Back work pulls the shoulders back and undoes desk and driving posture. Biceps get plenty of work from rows and pulldowns.
  • Day D, shoulders and arms with dumbbells. Three days after Monday's pressing, so the chest and triceps have recovered. Dumbbells force the small stabilizer muscles to work, which is a big part of what "functional" means. Loads are lighter; control is the point.
  • Day E, lower body with free weights and functional work. Three days after Tuesday. Hinge (kettlebell deadlift, Romanian deadlift), lunge, step-up, carry, sled. This is the day that transfers to lifting things, stairs, and getting up off the floor without thinking about it.
  • Core every day. The core recovers fast and yours needs the practice, so each day ends with two core moves (plus dead bug and glute bridge in the warm-up every session). The moves rotate: anti-rotation and plank on A, dead bug and bird dog on B, crunch and side plank on C, suitcase carry and back extension on D, farmer's carry and dead bug on E. Carries train the obliques (the love handle muscle) the way they are meant to work: resisting tipping, not twisting.

If a day gets missed, do not stack two days together; just do the next one in the rotation and let the week slide. The tracker always tells you which day is next. If you feel wrecked by Thursday in the first few weeks, drop Day D's weights by a third; the cardio hour still happens.

How to add weight

Each lift has a rep range, like 3 × 10 to 12. Start light enough that form is clean on every rep. When you hit the top of the range on all sets with good form two sessions in a row, add weight next time: 5 lb on upper body machines, 10 lb on leg machines, 10 to 20 lb on the leg press. If reps drop below the bottom of the range, the weight stays or goes down. The tracker shows your last numbers next to each lift so you always know what to beat.

Form rules that apply to everything

  • Set the seat and pads first. Joints line up with the machine's pivot point (usually marked with a red or yellow dot).
  • Lower the weight slower than you lift it. Two seconds down, one second up.
  • Brace before every rep: breathe into your belly, tighten like you are about to be poked in the stomach.
  • Last rep looks like the first rep. If it does not, the weight is too heavy.
  • No pain in a joint. Muscle burn is fine; sharp or pinching is a stop sign.

The cardio hour

One hour of aerobic work every gym day, 300 minutes a week. Most of it is at effort 5 or 6 out of 10: you can talk in short sentences but would not want to sing. That pace is what the visceral fat studies used. One day a week is intervals, because short harder efforts build fitness faster and hit glucose hard; the rest stays easy so you can do it five days running.

Which bike

Recumbent for the first month: it supports the back and does not load the hip flexors, which matters with yours tight. Move to the upright bike or a Stages cycle once an hour feels routine; they burn a bit more and work the core to hold you up. Seat height on any bike: knee almost straight at the bottom of the pedal stroke, a slight bend left.

Blood sugar and the gym

Exercise lowers blood sugar two ways: muscles pull in glucose directly while they work (no insulin needed), and for roughly a day afterward your body responds better to the insulin it has. Lifting adds muscle, which is more storage space for glucose. The hour of cardio and the lifting do different jobs, so keep both.

  • Check before. Under 100: eat 15 to 20 g of carbs (a banana, 4 glucose tabs, half a cup of juice) and wait 15 minutes. 100 to 250: go. Over 250 and you feel sick or have ketones: skip today and call your clinic.
  • Carry fast sugar in your gym bag. Shaky, sweaty, confused, or suddenly weak mid-workout means stop and treat it, then check.
  • Sauna and steam hide low-sugar symptoms (sweating, dizziness, racing heart). Do not go into either when you are under 100, and drink water before both.
  • Check after and log it. Over a few weeks the before and after chart tells you how your body responds, which is useful for your doctor.
  • Lows can show up hours later, including overnight, after a long session. Eat a real meal with protein within an hour of finishing.
  • If you take insulin or a sulfonylurea, ask your prescriber about dose timing around workouts before you start this plan. That is not something to guess at.

Weak core and tight hips

These two go together: tight hip flexors tilt the pelvis forward, which switches off the deep abdominals and loads the lower back. The fix is the same every session: stretch the front of the hip, then teach the core to hold the pelvis still (dead bug, bird dog, plank, Pallof press). Core work is in every lifting day on purpose. Do not skip it to save time; skip a set of curls instead.

What the research says for you

A 38 year old man with type 2 diabetes who wants to lose the love handles and build lean, functional muscle. Here is what the strongest studies found, and what each one changes in this plan.

  • Do both weights and cardio; neither alone is enough. In the DARE trial (251 adults with type 2 diabetes, 26 weeks), aerobic alone and weights alone each cut HbA1c about 0.5 points; doing both cut it 0.97. HART-D (262 adults, 9 months) found the same: only the combined group had a significant HbA1c drop, and it also lost the most waist (2.8 cm) and fat mass (1.7 kg). This is why every session here has lifts and a cardio hour.
  • The ADA's dose. At least 150 minutes a week of moderate to vigorous aerobic work on 3 or more days with no more than 2 days off in a row, plus resistance training 2 to 3 days a week on non-consecutive days, 8 to 10 exercises, 1 to 3 sets of 10 to 15 reps to near fatigue, adding load only once you consistently beat the target reps. Five sessions of 60 minutes puts you at 300 minutes, and the five-day rotation gives each muscle its 48 hours. The 15 minute walk after meals is a checkbox on the Today tab for the weekend.
  • Love handles cannot be targeted. A six week trial of 7 ab exercises, 5 days a week, improved curl-up endurance and changed abdominal fat by nothing. The core work here is for your weak core and your back, not for the fat on top of it. That fat comes off through total calorie balance and the cardio volume.
  • For the deep belly fat, cardio does the heavy lifting. Two meta-analyses (35 trials in the general population; 24 trials in type 2 diabetes) found aerobic exercise reduces visceral fat and resistance training alone does not. So the bike hour stays even after the lifting gets fun.
  • Weights are what change the shape. In HART-D the resistance and combined groups lost 1.4 and 1.7 kg of fat mass; aerobic alone lost 0.6 and was not different from control. Lifting is what keeps muscle while fat comes off, which is the difference between lean and just lighter.
  • Protein. A meta-analysis of 49 trials found muscle and strength gains from lifting rise with protein intake up to about 1.6 g per kg of body weight a day, then plateau. For a 210 lb (95 kg) man that is roughly 150 g a day, spread across meals, from food first. Ask your clinic if you have any kidney concern; otherwise this is a normal intake for someone lifting.
  • Hypoglycemia timing. Lows after exercise usually show up 6 to 15 hours later and can occur up to 48 hours out. Lifting before cardio (the order used here) helps blunt the drop. Exercising with high glucose and ketones is not recommended.

Sources: Sigal et al., Annals of Internal Medicine 2007 (DARE); Church et al., JAMA 2010 (HART-D); Colberg et al., Diabetes Care 2016 (ADA position statement); Vispute et al., J Strength Cond Res 2011; Ismail et al., Obesity Reviews 2012; Sabag et al., Diabetes and Metabolism 2017; Morton et al., Br J Sports Med 2018.
